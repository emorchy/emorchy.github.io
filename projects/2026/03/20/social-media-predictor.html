Social Media Predictor
20 Mar 2026

Goal: Attempt to predict YikYak post engagement from post text and auxiliary metadata (e.g. time, punctuation count, emoji usage) using a mix of classic MLPs and transformer models.

The documentation, scripts, and evaluation metrics can be found here.

Overview

YikYak is an anonymous messaging platform that allows college students to create and view discussions at UCSD. Posts and comments can be voted positively or negatively by the users, denoted as a “vote total” in the community. By leveraging Machine Learning (ML) and Natural Language Processing (NLP), previous messages can be used to predict the average vote count that a new post would receive.

Why?

Far too often, a user is concerned about whether a message will be well received in their community. Predicting vote totals can allow a user to alter their message to maximize user reachability and positive engagement.

How?

Dataset Retrieval

To train a model, I first had to scrape as many YikYak posts and comments from the UCSD group.

I created a scraped collection of posts and comments from UCSD’s YikYak group from March 30, 2023 to March 5, 2026. Without filtering null text and posts, the database snapshot contained 51,342 posts and 78,760 comments in total. Of these, 46,796 posts and 76,764 comments are text-only.

Post Statistics

Data Input/Output

Inputs: post text and created_at. Output: percentile (vote total). The target is likes_percentile in (0, 1): within each calendar week, I compute the mean and standard deviation of log(1 + likes), convert each post to a z-score within its week, then map the z-score through the standard normal CDF. This yields a comparable, stable scale across weeks and is invertible to log or raw likes using per-week statistics.

Auxiliary Features

There are 18 numeric features: day_of_week, hour_sin, hour_cos, text_length, num_words, num_hashtags, num_punctuation, is_question, caps_ratio, emoji_count, has_emoji, contains_number, time_since_last_post, week_index, weekly_post_count, log_weekly_post_count, week_log_like_mean, and week_log_like_std. They are standardized with StandardScaler fit on the training set only.

Splitting

By default, the split is strictly temporal (70% train, 15% val, 15% test). In an attempt to improve model accuracy, I also added an optional feature to randomly split. I also used a "latest fraction" (e.g., 0.6, 0.75, 0.8, 1.0) for ablations, as the oldest 10% of posts existed over 4 years ago and may have less relevance to the posts of today. During attempted classification, I added an option to balance classes, so each class would contain the equal amount of posts during training.

BERT Pipeline

BERT Pipeline Diagram

  1. DAPT: Masked language modeling on posts and comments (domain adaptation).
  2. TAPT: Masked LM on posts only, labels stripped (task adaptation).
  3. Supervised regression: Load the adapted BERT, add a regression head and auxiliary tower.
    1. Freeze BERT, train the MLP only. Important for incorporating auxiliary inputs and regression head.
    2. Optionally unfreeze all or top-k layers with a low learning rate. Differential learning rates are used for the transformer vs. the head. I unfroze the top 4 layers during phase B testing.

Models

Model Shapes

Leveraged multiple model types with various tests on their hyperparameters and configuration. The first model was a Multi-Layer Perceptron on the MiniLM embeddings with appended auxiliary inputs. The second model was an MLP with a small transformer layer after embedding. I also did end-to-end DistilBERT (tokenize → [CLS]+aux tower → head). These are reported for comparison only.

Evaluation

I report RMSE and MAE on the percentile target, fraction of predictions within 0.5σ and 1.0σ, and MAE on raw likes (inverted from percentile). For classification, I report accuracy and F1.

Target Choices

In an attempt to find the most accurate results, the pipeline supports multiple output styles. What began with simple log-vote regression changed into vote-based and percentile-based classification, then a single binary output, with and without sigmoid. The final regression target was decided to be a weekly normalized percentile. The target was found to be the most effective and is comparable across weeks for higher accuracy and variation accountability.

Model Comparison

TAPT epoch testing comparison: DAPT=3, latest 80% data, temporal split, frozen then unfrozen (top-4), early stopping

Analysis

The BERT pipeline (DAPT 3, TAPT 48, frozen 9, unfrozen 2 with 0.8 ablation and a temporal split) achieves Test RMSE 0.2626 and MAE 0.2191, with 42.17% of predictions within 0.5σ and 76.16% within 1.0σ, and MAE on raw likes 12.15. It improves over the baseline (Test RMSE 0.2952, MAE 0.2457) by roughly 11% relative on RMSE and MAE. Domain and task adaptation (DAPT then TAPT) plus frozen/unfrozen regression yield a model that generalizes to chronologically later data (0.8 temporal split). With max_length=64, only 0.4% of posts are truncated. Classification results with 10 frozen epochs and 2 unfrozen epochs reach 46.14% accuracy, 0.3476 F1, and 0.4444 weighted F1.

Evaluation
